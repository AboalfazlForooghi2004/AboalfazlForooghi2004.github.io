  <h1 class="case-study__title">About</h1>

  <div class="case-study__section">
    <h2 class="case-study__section-title">Professional Background</h2>
    <p>
      I'm a Network Engineer with hands-on experience designing, implementing, and
      supporting network infrastructure. My work spans enterprise networking with
      Cisco and MikroTik equipment — routing, switching, firewalls, VPNs — and
      increasingly focuses on Linux-based networking, network virtualization, and
      automation.
    </p>
    <p>
      I work with traditional routing and switching protocols, but my primary interest
      is in using Linux as a networking platform: VRF for routing isolation, network
      namespaces for stack virtualization, Open vSwitch for software-defined switching,
      nftables for policy enforcement, and FRRouting for BGP and dynamic routing
      on Linux hosts.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="case-study__section">
    <h2 class="case-study__section-title">Technical Interests</h2>
    <ul class="case-study__list">
      <li>Linux networking: VRF, network namespaces, nftables, policy-based routing, traffic control</li>
      <li>Software-defined networking: Open vSwitch, virtual switching, tenant isolation, VLAN architecture</li>
      <li>Routing protocols: BGP with FRRouting, multi-path routing, route maps and filtering</li>
      <li>Network automation: Python scripting for device configuration, Ansible for infrastructure as code</li>
      <li>Network monitoring: Flask-based dashboards, NetFlow analysis, FortiGate log ingestion</li>
      <li>Infrastructure: Docker/Podman, Linux server hardening, DNS/DHCP services</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div class="case-study__section">
    <h2 class="case-study__section-title">Engineering Philosophy</h2>
    <p>
      I believe in understanding how things work at the packet level, not just at the
      configuration level. Building network virtualization labs on Linux has taught me
      more about routing, isolation, and policy enforcement than any documentation
      alone could. I value reproducible experiments, honest documentation of what works
      and what doesn't, and sharing knowledge through clear technical writing.
    </p>
    <p>
      I distinguish clearly between what I've implemented and tested, what I'm actively
      learning, and what I'm interested in exploring. I don't claim expertise I don't
      have, and I treat every lab experiment as an opportunity to understand a system
      more deeply.
    </p>
  </div>
